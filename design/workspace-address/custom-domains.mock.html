Workspace settings › Custom domains

Where a workspace owner or admin connects a domain they own so that it serves the workspace's hosted application. A domain belongs to the workspace; there is no primary address and no redirect between a customer's domains, and removing one reserves nothing. Scenario: the Acme workspace, whose application shop is live at shop--acme.motir.site. Today is 30 October 2026 in every panel.

Panel 6 — unverified · a subdomain and an apex

The DNS records panel opens on its own for an unverified domain. The ownership TXT comes with the pointing record, which differs by shape: a subdomain takes a CNAME, a root domain takes A and AAAA. A root under a multi-label public suffix (acme.co.uk) is a root and is never offered a CNAME. The A value is a shared Fly IPv4 and is labelled so.

shop.acme.com Unverified

Create these 2 records at your DNS provider, then check.

Type Name Value
CNAME shop motir-edge.fly.dev
TXT _motir-verify.shop motir-verify-Q2x…9fA
acme.com Unverified

Create these 3 records at your DNS provider, then check.

Type Name Value
A @ 66.241.125.217
AAAA @ 2a09:8280:1::17d:93fd:0
TXT _motir-verify motir-verify-k7P…2cB

A root domain cannot take a CNAME, so it points at Motir's addresses. The IPv4 address is shared with other Motir domains; if it ever changes we will tell you before it does.

Panel 9 — the three failures, each with the record and one action

The Failure banner says what happened, shows the exact record or value to restore, and offers one action. Its hue is in the callout's border and tint; the words stay on the strong ink. It is announced (role="alert") only when the status changes to a failure, not on every load. The domain does not serve while failed.

shop.acme.com Failed
We can no longer see your ownership record Restore this TXT record at your DNS provider: _motir-verify.shop.acme.com
motir-verify-Q2x…9fA
acme.com Failed
acme.com no longer points at Motir The A record now answers 203.0.113.9. Set it back to: A @ 66.241.125.217
AAAA @ 2a09:8280:1::17d:93fd:0
blog.acme.com Failed
The certificate could not be issued The certificate authority said: CAA record for acme.com does not allow letsencrypt.org

A subdomain's pointing failure names its CNAME the same way: "The CNAME record now answers other-host.example. Set it back to: CNAME shop motir-edge.fly.dev". The three failure kinds — ownership record lost, pointing record lost, issuance failed — are the reasons the status job reports; the third carries the platform's reason text verbatim.

Panel 11 — remove · the confirm, removing, and a failed remove

The Remove confirm says the domain stops answering at once, that nothing is reserved, and that the DNS records are the customer's to delete. On success the row leaves the list and a toast confirms. On failure the confirm stays open with the error and a Retry; the row is unchanged.

Remove shop.acme.com?

It stops serving your application at once. Nothing is reserved: you can add it again later, and another workspace can add it if it proves ownership. The DNS records at your provider are yours to delete.
