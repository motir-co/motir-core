0 · Where it sits: a slice of the roadmap canvas at 1×, two rows of the real layout

The door is the roadmap itself: the card the level marks “here” (the in-progress row with the smallest depth, pickHereId) wears the tab. The slice uses the layout’s real spacing — 280 × 124 cards, 80px between columns, 72px between rows. The card above is selected, so its View / Open slot hangs 14px into the gap; the here card is hovered, so its next-action button hangs on its top edge at the right. The tab takes the top-left 20px of the same gap: 38px of clear band remains between the slot above and the tab. A single edge entering the top lands at the card’s middle, clear of the tab.

          4 · Every data-style, light: the here card hovered, so the tab and the next-action button are seen together

              6 · Zoomed out: the tab scales with the card and keeps its words

              The canvas has no level-of-detail switch: the world transform scales every card whole, from 2× down to MIN_SCALE 0.3. The tab scales with it and keeps its text, as every tag on the card does. Below about 60% its words stop being legible, as the status pill’s do, and what carries “here” is the solid accent block on the card’s top-left edge plus the accent frame — the one filled block on any card at that position, so it still reads at 30%. Collapsing to the pin glyph would need a zoom-dependent render the canvas does not have for any other element, and would save nothing: the block is the signal at that size, not the glyph inside it. The Locate control (LOCATE snaps to 1× on the here card) is the way back to reading it.

                7 · Alternatives weighed and rejected

                A · An extra top row inside the card is not drawn as a card: it cannot be built inside the fixed 124px box without taking a title line (the title’s last line ends at 103px today, so a 20px row leaves room for two lines of title, not three), and the layout spaces rows by NODE_H, so growing the box for one card is not available either. B · A pin badge on the top-left corner alone (drawn below) is legible zoomed out and costs no width, but it is a glyph with no words: “you are here” is the canvas’s orientation cue for a reader who has just arrived, and the map-pin alone does not say it to someone who has not learned it. It also sits on the card’s corner radius, where every style draws the corner differently. The tab keeps the words and costs nothing inside the card.

                B · pin badge only (rejected)
